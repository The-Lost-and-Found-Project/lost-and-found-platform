# Owner-assigned role training casebook

Status: synthetic practice material for the draft curricula in owner-role-training-drafts.md. These examples are not real member submissions and do not establish live moderation policy. Replace or supplement the decision criteria only after Owners approve the relevant policy and escalation route.

## How to use the casebook

Learners work through each case before the facilitator explains the expected boundary. The facilitator asks: What do you know? What is outside your authority? What is the safest next step? Learners must explain their reasoning, not guess a preferred answer.

Use only the public visitor prayer workflow during moderator practice. Never enter real prayer requests, contact details, care notes, or member profile information into a training environment. If an approved moderation standard or escalation contact is missing, the learner holds the case and the role remains inactive until that prerequisite is completed.

## Moderator practice cases

### M1 — Visitor submission with no obvious privacy issue

**Synthetic submission:** “Please pray for my family while we face a difficult season.”

**Discussion:** Is this a visitor submission? Is it marked public and pending? What does the approved moderation checklist require before an outcome?

**Expected boundary:** Verify the item is in the assigned visitor queue and apply the approved checklist. Do not treat a sympathetic or familiar subject as automatic approval. If the checklist does not answer the case, hold and escalate.

### M2 — Private member request encountered elsewhere

**Synthetic scenario:** While working in another admin area, the learner notices a member prayer request marked private.

**Expected boundary:** Do not open, search, copy, change, or share it. The moderator workflow does not authorize access to member requests. If the system exposes private content in the moderation queue, stop and report the access issue through the approved route.

### M3 — Visitor submission includes identifying details

**Synthetic submission:** “Please pray for Jordan Smith at 555-0100 while he is recovering.”

**Discussion:** Can the moderator edit out the name or phone number and publish the rest?

**Expected boundary:** No. The moderator cannot edit the submitter's words or broaden/change an audience. Hold and escalate under the approved privacy procedure. Do not copy the identifying details into a separate report; use the approved event-reporting mechanism.

### M4 — Conflict of interest

**Synthetic scenario:** The learner recognizes the visitor submission as being about a close relative.

**Expected boundary:** Disclose the conflict and do not make the decision. Use the approved reassignment/escalation path. Never ask another person to approve outside their authorized scope.

### M5 — Unclear or sensitive content

**Synthetic submission:** The message is ambiguous and the learner is unsure whether it meets the approved public criteria.

**Expected boundary:** Hold/escalate. Do not infer facts, investigate the submitter, counsel, diagnose, promise a ministry response, or publish while uncertain. If the moderation guide lacks the relevant rule, document that the policy gap must be resolved before the queue can be safely delegated.

### M6 — Possible urgent safety concern

**Synthetic scenario:** A visitor submission appears to describe immediate danger.

**Expected boundary:** Do not improvise a crisis response, promise confidentiality, or treat ordinary moderation as crisis care. Follow the approved safety escalation protocol. If no protocol or contact is approved, stop and escalate to an Owner; do not activate moderators until this protocol exists.

## Content Creator practice cases

### C1 — Build from the passage

**Assignment:** Prepare a short study outline from an assigned passage using the supplied source packet.

**Expected evidence:** The outline begins with the passage and its context; distinguishes text, explanation, interpretation, and application; identifies sources; and includes questions that let learners observe and discuss before the facilitator explains.

### C2 — Language claim without context

**Synthetic draft sentence:** “This Greek word always means one specific kind of love.”

**Discussion:** What evidence would make this claim responsible?

**Expected boundary:** Do not rely on an isolated gloss or claim a word has one fixed meaning in every passage. Check the source, grammatical and literary context, translation choices, and the word's use in this passage. Revise or remove any unsupported claim and flag uncertainty for review.

### C3 — Source and copyright boundary

**Synthetic scenario:** A learner copies several paragraphs from a modern commentary into a study draft without permission or attribution.

**Expected boundary:** Do not submit the copied text as original work. Remove it from the draft, identify the source, and use only material the ministry is authorized to use. Follow the approved citation and copyright checklist; ask a reviewer when permission is unclear.

### C4 — Member information in a draft

**Synthetic scenario:** The creator wants to illustrate a lesson with a recognizable member prayer story.

**Expected boundary:** Do not include member prayer, care, or profile information. Replace it with a fully fabricated example or an approved source. A role in content creation gives no access or permission to reuse member information.

### C5 — Assigned collection and draft-only authority

**Synthetic scenario:** The creator has an assignment in Collection A, sees a draft in Collection B, and has a visible Publish control.

**Expected boundary:** Work only in the explicitly assigned collection. Do not open or edit another creator's work without separate authority. Submit drafts for review; do not publish, approve, or archive. A visible control does not grant permission.

## Verifier scoring guide

Score each item as **meets**, **revise**, or **not demonstrated**. The learner must meet every safety and scope boundary; a single unsafe choice on private content, audience changes, conflicts, unauthorized sources, member data, or publishing requires a revise result and another supervised session.

- Identifies the authorized scope and the facts that still need checking.
- Chooses only an allowed action and stops when policy or authority is unclear.
- Explains the privacy, Scripture, or source boundary in plain language.
- Uses the approved escalation, review, and audit route without adding sensitive content.
- Demonstrates a respectful, participant-centered teaching style for content drafts.
- Accepts feedback and can revise the work while preserving source notes.

The verifier records casebook version, cases used, scoring, feedback, outcome, and verifier identity. Training evidence remains separate from the role request and does not grant access. No real member data is included in the evidence packet.

## Finalization checklist

- Owners approve the public moderation criteria and safety escalation protocol before using M1–M6 as pass/fail moderation checks.
- Owners approve the source, citation, translation, and copyright checklist before using C1–C3 for activation.
- Confirm how the draft-only interface and assigned collection will be demonstrated without granting live access.
- Version this casebook alongside the corresponding session plan and name its verifier.
