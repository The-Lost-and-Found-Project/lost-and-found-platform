# L&F rebuild design preview

This first implementation package adds `/rebuild-preview` as a public, read-only design sample. It does not replace the public home, member dashboard, or current administration screens. The route is intentionally available without sign-in because the preview deployment uses isolated synthetic authentication that cannot accept real L&F passwords.

The preview contains three switchable surfaces:

- Administration: the approved cream sidebar, compact overview, attention queue, schedule, a role-aware master calendar, free event registration and review, an assignment and role-based work queue, a Scripture-centered Studies workspace, a sample People & roles workspace, a sample Prayer review workspace, and notification and profile-sharing settings.
- Public homepage: the approved typography-led direction with Luke 19:10 as the focal Scripture.
- Member home: personal study continuation, groups, commitments, a personal calendar, and a structured request for a private ministry-leader check-in.

All counts, events, studies, registrations, role requests, tasks, and assignments are synthetic design fixtures. The Events view shows sample gatherings, free registration counts, and unpublished drafts; its planner has no payment field. Studies presents a sample library, group pacing, and an Emmaus connection view. It reflects the existing connected Emmaus sign-in and shows study sharing/progress sync as a design question, without reading or changing Emmaus content or progress. The My tasks workspace separates assigned work from queues available through approved roles, with role filters, due cues, and direct task entry points. Member home shows only personal assignments and includes a private care check-in prototype. Members choose whether they are doing okay, could use prayer, or want a personal check-in. The optional note is shown only as local sample state; the preview never sends it. The planned audience is authorized leaders assigned to care for that member. In the administration task workspace, assigned care follow-ups appear only when the Ministry leader role filter is selected. The sample queue includes a member-requested check-in and a regular leader check-in reminder; private notes are omitted. Reminders support follow-through without scoring people. The sample follow-up uses an existing trusted channel rather than member-to-member messaging. The leader can mark a sample follow-up planned or complete; those status changes stay local and are not saved. It is not a public prayer post or member chat. The People & roles screen includes a searchable shared-profile directory that lists only members who opted into sharing their directory name, plus role requests, scoped delegation, and current access; it preserves the existing Supervisor lane and Owner-only administrator boundary. Directory cards reveal only sample fields marked as shared, and contact fields remain hidden unless individually shared. Prayer review separates visitor submissions awaiting public clearance, member requests with member-selected audiences, and cleared public requests. The public homepage opens a visitor prayer form that requires acknowledgement of public sharing after review; it offers no private audience. Member home opens a form defaulted to Prayer team only, with accepted-group and public-after-review options. Moderators cannot broaden a member-selected audience. Both forms are sample-only and never send or save the entered text. Notification settings show every category enabled by default, individual opt-outs, and a sample sign-in push prompt with Enable and Not now choices. Profile sharing keeps each field private by default, gives contact fields independent switches, and previews the fields visible to the selected audience. Account & data previews a personal-data export and an account-removal confirmation that includes L&F and connected Emmaus account data in its planned checklist. Its typed confirmation only previews the final step; it does not export or delete data. The calendar workspace combines master or personal scope with category, ministry, and role filters. Sample entries show which approved roles can see an event and who is assigned; an expandable guide explains that assigned access and approved role access remain distinct. These are presentation samples, not authorization. Visitor submissions are public-only; member privacy is never broadened by moderation. Preview buttons display explanations. There are no event registration, submission, approval, membership, preference, account, export, deletion, payment, or content writes. The push prompt does not invoke the browser permission API. The client has no API calls or browser storage access. No new dependency or paid service is introduced.

The shared app frame omits its existing header, footer, bottom navigation, floating actions, and push-subscription sync on this exact preview route to avoid duplicate navigation and automatic subscription side effects. Existing routes retain their behavior. The preview is marked noindex.

## Review flow

1. Open `/rebuild-preview` on the preview deployment; no account is needed.
2. Compare Administration, Public homepage, and Member home using the clearly labeled review controls.
3. On a narrow screen, open and close Menu. Confirm there is no horizontal page scrolling and only one application menu.
4. Open Members → Member directory. Search the sample names and confirm only opted-in directory names and shared fields appear.
5. Open Role requests, Delegation, and Current access. Verify the Owner-only administrator boundary is visible.
6. Open Ministry tools → Events. Review Upcoming, Registrations, and Drafts. Open the planner and confirm the flow has a free-participation note and no payment field.
7. Open Ministry tools → Studies. Compare Study library, Groups & pacing, and Emmaus connection. Confirm the connected login is acknowledged and progress sync is shown as a separate design item.
8. Open My tasks. Compare All work, Assigned to me, and Role queues; narrow by role and confirm the member view contains only personal assignments.
9. Open Calendars. Change master/personal scope, category filter, ministry, and role. Verify combined filtering, role and assignment labels, the sharing guide, and the empty-result state.
10. Open section information icons. Confirm the explanation remains optional and readable.
11. Open sample review actions. Verify that an explanatory message appears and no real approval is performed.
12. Open Settings → Notifications. Confirm all five categories start on, can be turned off individually, and the push prompt offers Enable and Not now without triggering a browser permission request.
13. Open Settings → Profile sharing. Confirm every field starts private, contact fields are independent, and the preview reflects only selected fields.
14. Open Settings → Account & data. Preview an export, open account removal, and verify the final-step control remains disabled until REMOVE is typed. Confirm no data is exported or deleted.
15. Open Ministry tools → Prayer review. Switch among Visitor requests, Member requests, and Cleared public. Confirm pending visitor requests are hidden from the public, and the member audience stays Prayer team only.
16. On Public homepage, open Share a prayer request. Confirm the public-only explanation, required acknowledgement, and disabled submit until both fields are complete. Close without submitting.
17. On Member home, open Share a prayer request. Confirm Prayer team only is selected by default; inspect the accepted-group and public-after-review options and the moderator audience rule. Do not submit real content.
18. On Public homepage, review Prayer & praise. Confirm only sample items marked cleared are displayed and no names or real requests appear.
19. On Member home, open Request a check-in. Confirm the three response choices, optional note, explicit authorized-leader audience, and disabled submit until a choice and acknowledgement are selected. Close without submitting.
20. In Administration → My tasks, choose Ministry leader and confirm the assigned care follow-ups include a member-requested check-in and a regular leader check-in reminder, with no private note in the queue. Set a sample follow-up to planned and complete; confirm the status is not saved. No message is sent.
21. Exit the preview to return to existing administration.

## Validation

Five focused regression checks cover calendar-scope composition, combined filtering, fixture immutability, public noindex route behavior, and absence of client-side production writes. TypeScript and focused ESLint checks are run separately from the repository's build configuration, which currently ignores TypeScript build errors.

On the unchanged `origin/main` baseline, the full unit suite has eight failures, including a malformed active-guard test and outdated source-string assertions. The preview branch has the same eight failures and adds five passing checks. These baseline failures must not be described as a passing full suite.

Local Chromium failed to launch in this workspace. The public sample was reviewed in a connected browser: Members directory search, My tasks in administration and member views, calendar role filtering, Studies library/group/Emmaus views, Prayer review, People & roles, notifications, and profile sharing fit a 390px viewport without horizontal page overflow. The review confirmed that names not opted into the directory are excluded, selected member fields alone appear, member task queues exclude role approvals, and the existing Emmaus login is noted while study/progress sync remains unimplemented. All data is synthetic. No real browser notification permission was requested, and no production data fixture is included in this change.

The member care check-in and assigned follow-up views remain design prototypes. They do not persist private information, implement real care-role access, or send a message. The calendar view remains a design prototype: it does not create events, configure real sharing, enforce access, or connect to member calendars. Delegated approvals, privacy sharing persistence, event registration, role expansion, and cross-application progress are not implemented by this visual preview.
